# Slop Points

Slop Points record participation and accepted contributions. Points have no
monetary value and cannot be transferred or redeemed. They confer no payment,
ownership, governance, or access entitlement. Only accepted-work points for a project and payment period determine its reward
shares under the published payment rules. Payout-recognition bonuses never
affect payouts. Points this month and total points are views of
the same history, not separate currencies. Historical score fields encode
accepted-work points at 30 points per score (10 per third); they are not a
second public metric. Preserve historical settlements and funded obligations.

## Discontinued participation awards

From 24 October 2026 (PRD section 11, owner decision D2), welcome and X
connection awards are discontinued for everyone. Joining and connecting X earn
no points. Awards recorded before that date stay in the append-only journal
unchanged, but no displayed total, balance, standing, or ranking counts them.
Never delete, rewrite, or reissue them. Descriptions of those awards below are
historical. Migration `0080_points_participation_discontinued.sql` makes new
memberships record no welcome award and has the database refuse any new
welcome or X connection award.

## Earning and history

Joining with a GitHub user account earned five welcome points once until
24 October 2026; it now earns none. Existing
contributors receive contribution points without joining or registering a
wallet. Joining is dated when it occurs; historical work retains its actual
GitHub occurrence date, with a separate points-recording timestamp. GitHub
sign-in proves account control, not one unique person per account.

Tiered accepted work earns ten points per unweighted score-third: micro 10,
small 30, medium 90, large 240, XL 450, exceptional 750. Eligible standard
reviews earn 30; from 2026-10-01 an ordinary review earns 10 only as the one
outcome review of its pull request; reviewed triage, deep, and specialist reviews earn 10, 90,
and 240. Legacy integer score events earn 30 points per unweighted score.
Verified evaluated contributions follow that same unweighted conversion.
Token usage, cost, model, signed-receipt weight, wallet state, and payment never
multiply points. Required source evidence and project participation rules apply.

Historical merged PRs initially receive 10 provisional points each, including
work before the project's payment program. A Score v2 merge from
2026-10-01T00:00:00Z without a ratified tier is provisional small credit and
receives 30 provisional points in the legacy projection. When source-bound value
assessments are enabled, missing, stale, incomplete, or unresolved assessments
do not create fallback awards or remove prior recorded points. A verified bot
assessment remains provisional until a maintainer decision supersedes it. An
assessed zero creates a zero-amount revision. This is a recognition backfill and
never retroactive cash eligibility. Verified archived and current scoring
records supersede provisional amounts, including exclusions and work grouping
within their complete source windows. Historical review credit is limited to
verified scoring/evaluation records; the history download publishes its actual
coverage. Recorded points do not claim a complete lifetime review census.

A logical accepted work unit earns once. Ordinary reviews do not also earn an
evaluated review award on the same PR. Bots, self-review, unaccepted work and
unreviewed external activity do not earn contribution points. Maintainers retain
work acceptance and correction authority. Automated suspicion is not an award,
ban, or final exclusion. Contest an award through the project's GitHub review
process; never put private security evidence in a public appeal.

## Display

Recorded totals persist between months, subject to explicit corrections.
Recorded totals exclude historical welcome and X connection awards. Monthly earned-point standings use UTC occurrence dates, include verified payout points,
and give equal totals equal ranks. Newcomer standings use the first recorded
earning event within the last 30 days. Badges recognize the first
accepted contribution, first qualifying review, and work in three projects.
They grant no additional points or benefits. Verified finalized payouts also receive a recognition badge. There are no referral commissions,
attendance rewards, multipliers, countdowns, purchase or transfer controls.

Balances have timestamps and explicit coverage. Missing or invalid data is
unavailable, never a fabricated zero. Unknown earning tiers display as points
after acceptance rather than a guaranteed numeric promise.

## Persistence and publication

`data/points/history.json` is the reviewed initial backfill, generated by
`scripts/backfill-points.ts`. The collector enumerates complete GitHub repository
connections and verifies the pre-cutoff immutable PR ID set in a second pass.
`data/points/backfill-report.json` records source counts and coverage. Public
GitHub review bodies are processed in memory and never copied to these files.
Projects and repositories come only from the canonical manifest registry.

`scripts/generate-points.ts` appends verified scoring/evaluation revisions,
retaining earlier revisions and source digests. Source IDs, actor IDs and
categories determine award identity; a rule upgrade cannot mint another copy.
Revisions carry predecessor hashes. A corrected outcome replaces its former
amount in the projection rather than adding another full award. Source-bound
score decisions also record the decision source, effective time, reason, and
exact signed change from the previous amount. A new decision with the same
amount records a zero change; replaying that decision creates no revision.
Distinct assessed review findings retain their separate work-unit identities.

The permanent D1 points tables are logically separate from trace tables in the
existing deployment database. This avoids a new credential or provisioning
boundary; the points handler never constructs trace persistence or reads trace
records. Journal rows reject updates, deletions, changed-row replacement, and
branching successors. Completed batch metadata is immutable. Exact imports and
replays preserve the original bytes. A batch
is publishable only when its full revision count reconciles. Trusted Actions
checkpoints the tested journal into D1 during every protected production
release, including the scheduled ones, under the existing publication lock.
Every online build verifies that the published
journal and D1 checkpoint share the exact same history prefix, then retains the
longer journal; divergent or unavailable history blocks publication. Public journal
reads are bounded, paginated and pinned to one completed batch. A failed import
cannot publish a partial batch.

The `slop-points-web` identity audience is distinct from `private-trace-api`.
Assertions are single use and exchanged for a one-hour HttpOnly, Secure,
SameSite=Strict web session. Mutations require a same-origin JSON request.
Membership is unique by immutable GitHub node and numeric IDs. Sessions cannot
create arbitrary awards, choose another actor, read traces, or ingest history.
Signout invalidates the session and never resets or reissues a historical welcome award.

A member can close the account from Account after typing their GitHub login
(`POST /api/v1/points/account/close`, migration 0063). One atomic change marks
the membership closed and private, ends every session, removes the X and
Telegram links and pending flows, and turns off optional activity email. The
membership row, welcome and connection awards, X and Telegram claims, points
history, wallets, unpaid obligations, payment and settlement records, private
cases, inbox rows and public GitHub work stay. The database refuses a session
or public visibility for a closed account. A repeated request that raced
the first closure reports that same closure. An ordinary sign-in to a closed
account returns `account_closed`; only a sign-in that sends `reopen: true`
opens it again, private and without connections. Support handles only lost
GitHub access and contested recovery.

New join-only membership is private unless opted into public display. Public
GitHub contribution history remains public. Visibility can be changed without
rejoining. Identity aliases never move balances between account IDs. A disputed
or reused login must resolve through verified identity rather than name matching.

## Operations and rollback

Generate a fresh complete ledger, then generate points. Scheduled updates must
load the completed D1 checkpoint and published shard journal first; failure blocks publication rather
than falling back to the initial seed. An explicit bootstrap is permitted only
for the initial code release with no completed journal. Offline builds use the
reviewed seed and available ledger and are not production freshness evidence.

Keep the journal on rollback. Disable points mutations or withdraw an affected
projection through a reviewed main release; never delete history or reissue
welcome awards. Re-enable only after exact replay and reward-isolation tests.
Measure first accepted outcomes, returning contributors, reviewer workload,
source duplication, corrections, and publication delay, not points minted.

## X connections and payout recognition

Until 24 October 2026, the first verified X connection earned 10 points once
per GitHub account; connections now earn no points (see Discontinued
participation awards).
The X numeric ID is reserved to its verified GitHub identity to prevent reuse
across accounts. A member can reconnect or switch X accounts. Disconnecting
removes the visible link while preserving historical award uniqueness.
Historical X awards were participation points, not accepted-work score.
Public community cards include opted-in members even without accepted work.

X uses OAuth 2.0 authorization code with S256 PKCE and a confidential client.
The browser flow cookie is separate, HttpOnly/Secure/SameSite=Lax, expires after
ten minutes, and is bound to the initiating GitHub web session. Callback state
is single-use. The application verifies `/2/users/me`, stores only account ID,
handle and verification time, and attempts to revoke the temporary provider
token immediately; provider failure never causes a token to be persisted.
No refresh token, posts, messages, follower lists, or provider token is retained.
The required read scopes are `tweet.read users.read`; posting, follow actions,
and messaging permissions are not requested. X handles are observed snapshots;
reconnect to refresh a changed handle. X control does not confer GitHub project,
organization, wallet, or payment authority.

Membership and X visibility each require opt-in. Both must be public before an
X link appears to others. Changing either preference hides the link. Signing
out cancels session-bound linking; connecting does not publish any post or DM.

Each recipient earns **25 points per project and finalized monthly payout
cycle** with positive paid principal. Dollar size, fee size, separate reward
lines, and transaction splitting never multiply this award. The immutable key
contains project, cycle and GitHub actor ID, and the occurrence date is the
verified settlement date. Proposed, approved, scheduled, held, unclaimed,
partial, and external-prize projections do not earn payout points. Existing
paid cycles are backfilled from complete validated lifecycle records; trusted
online generation rechecks finalized chain evidence before publishing new
awards. Receiving recognition never changes financial allocations or fees.
A wallet address alone does not identify a GitHub payer, so no payer identity
or organization-to-person relationship is invented.

### Enabling X

Create or use an X confidential Web App with OAuth 2.0. Register each intended
callback exactly (no wildcard):

- `https://slop.cash/api/v1/points/x/callback`
- `https://slop.tech/api/v1/points/x/callback`
- `https://eliza.army/api/v1/points/x/callback` (only if supporting the alias)

Place `X_CLIENT_ID` and `X_CLIENT_SECRET` together in the protected GitHub
production environment. The existing protected main release installs them
as encrypted Pages secrets and applies migration `0009_points_social.sql`.
Unattended schedules do not provision credentials. With neither configured,
the app reports that X connections are not enabled; partial configuration is
rejected. Provider account/API access and a real callback must be verified at
activation; tests with provider doubles are not live OAuth evidence.

Primary references checked during implementation:
[X authorization and PKCE](https://docs.x.com/fundamentals/authentication/oauth-2-0/authorization-code),
[X token exchange and revocation](https://docs.x.com/fundamentals/authentication/oauth-2-0/user-access-token),
[X endpoint scope mapping](https://docs.x.com/fundamentals/authentication/guides/v2-authentication-mapping).

### Telegram connections

A member may connect one Telegram account from a signed-in GitHub web session.
**A Telegram connection earns no points.** Like X, the numeric Telegram account
ID is reserved to the first GitHub identity that verifies it and never moves
to another account; reconnecting replaces the member's own link, and
disconnecting removes the visible link while keeping the reservation.

The browser never talks to Telegram and the site loads no Telegram script or
frame. `POST /api/v1/points/telegram/start` records a single-use code bound to
the web session, valid for ten minutes, and returns the deep link
`https://t.me/<bot>?start=<code>`. The member opens it and presses Start.
Telegram delivers that `/start <code>` message to
`POST /api/v1/points/telegram/webhook`, authenticated by the
`X-Telegram-Bot-Api-Secret-Token` header set at `setWebhook`; that delivery is
the proof of the account ID. The webhook accepts only a `/start` message from a
non-bot user in a private chat, replies only in that chat (connected, link no
longer valid, account already connected elsewhere, or how to start), and
answers 200 after authentication so a consumed code is never retried. Any
other update is ignored without a reply. The bot never joins groups, posts, or
reads anything beyond that message.

Stored: account ID, the optional username snapshot, verification time and
visibility. Not stored: phone number, display name, photo, chat history. The
numeric ID is never shown to others; public display uses the username
snapshot only, and `Telegram · connected` when the account has none. Public
display needs both the public membership and the public link opt-in, as with X.
Telegram control confers no GitHub project, organization, wallet or payment
authority, and Telegram failure never blocks GitHub work or payments.

### Enabling Telegram

Create a bot with BotFather and keep its token private. Register the webhook
once against the production origin, with a fresh random secret of at least 32
characters from `A-Za-z0-9_-`:

```text
POST https://api.telegram.org/bot<token>/setWebhook
{ "url": "https://slop.cash/api/v1/points/telegram/webhook",
  "secret_token": "<secret>", "allowed_updates": ["message"],
  "drop_pending_updates": true }
```

Place `TELEGRAM_BOT_TOKEN`, `TELEGRAM_BOT_USERNAME` (the bot's username
without `@`) and `TELEGRAM_WEBHOOK_SECRET` together in the protected GitHub
production environment. The protected main release installs the three as
encrypted Pages secrets and applies migration `0056_points_telegram.sql`.
With none configured, the app reports that Telegram connections are not
enabled and the webhook answers 404; a partial or malformed set is rejected at
release and disables the routes. A real bot and a live webhook delivery must
be verified at activation; tests with doubles are not live evidence.

Primary references checked during implementation:
[Bot deep links](https://core.telegram.org/api/links#bot-links),
[setWebhook and the secret token](https://core.telegram.org/bots/api#setwebhook),
[Update and Message objects](https://core.telegram.org/bots/api#update).

### Individual profiles

Every recorded PR author has a discoverable `/contributors/<login>` profile,
including authors with only open or closed unmerged PRs. Joined members and
historical point recipients retain profiles independently of the current score
window. Public membership, X visibility and Telegram visibility remain opt-in.

`profiles:generate` walks the complete PR connection of each active manifest
repository, deduplicates immutable PR IDs, and reconciles the total record count
before publishing `public/data/profiles.json`. Deleted authors and non-user
actors remain in source counts but are excluded from the human directory.
Merged, open, and closed-without-merge are distinct counts. These are Slop
repository totals, not claims about an account's entire GitHub history. Scan
start/end times and stale data are displayed; failures do not become zeroes.
Trusted releases refresh the census. The reviewed `data/profiles/seed.json`
provides a dated development/PR bootstrap; it is not a live-data claim.

Profiles use GitHub's public avatar with an initials fallback. GitHub and
verified opt-in X links are shown without implying organization authority.
Verified cycle receipts use immutable actor IDs and integer USDC micro-units.
Direct-payment disclosures appear as a separate reported-transfer total with
transaction links; they never become verified settlement records or inflate
the verified receipt total. Estimates and points are not money received.
